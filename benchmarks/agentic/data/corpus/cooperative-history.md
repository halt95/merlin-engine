# History of the Lume Valley Rail Cooperative

The Lume Valley Rail Cooperative was founded in 1874 at a meeting of mill owners, farmers and
quarrymen in the corn exchange at Holm Cross. Members bought shares at five crowns each, and each
member had one vote regardless of the number of shares held.

The founding chair was Edda Rensvik, a surveyor born in Fallowmere in 1831. She had mapped the
valley for the county and drew the first route. She chaired the cooperative from its founding until
1889.

Construction began in 1875. The first line, from Brennick to Holm Cross, opened in 1877. The line was
extended down the valley to the harbour at Oarside in 1883; the extension needed the Lume Viaduct
near Kestrel Wood. The upland branch from Holm Cross to Fallowmere opened in 1896, after a long
dispute over the gradient (see rolling-stock).

The second chair, Tobin Achterberg, a Brennick mill manager, served from 1889 to 1907. Under him the
cooperative opened its own engine works and doubled the harbour traffic. He was followed by Ilse
Varga's colleague Moss Elderkin, a signal engineer, who served from 1907 to 1919.

In 1908 the members amended the bylaws to limit how long a chair may serve (see bylaws).

The cooperative still owns the line. A later timetable from 1925 is mentioned in timetable-1902.
