# The Lume Viaduct and the 1911 survey

The Lume Viaduct carries the main line across the Lume gorge near Kestrel Wood. The original viaduct,
opened with the Oarside extension in 1883, was an iron truss bridge of 9 spans with a total length of
412 m.

In 1911 the engineer Ilse Varga surveyed the viaduct and found serious corrosion in the lower chords
of the iron trusses. Her report recommended a speed limit of 25 km/h at once and a full rebuild.

The rebuild ran from 1913 to 1915. The new viaduct is a steel bridge of 11 spans with a total length
of 431 m; it is longer than the original because the southern abutment was moved back from the river
bank, which had been eroding. During the rebuild trains crossed on a single line at walking pace.

With the new southern abutment in place, the cooperative opened a small halt at Lume Bridge in 1916.

The survey also measured the height of the rail above normal river level as 38 m, unchanged by the
rebuild.
