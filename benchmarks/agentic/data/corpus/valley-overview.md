# The Lume Valley: an overview

This corpus describes the Lume Valley, a fictional river valley invented for this benchmark. None of
its places, people or figures are real, so a model can only answer questions about it by reading the
documents.

The River Lume rises on Fallowmere Fells and runs 96 km south-west to the sea at Oarside. Four towns
matter to the valley's history.

**Brennick** is the largest town and the valley's administrative seat. The 1910 census counted
41,200 residents. Brennick grew around woollen mills, and the mills were the first large customers of
the railway.

**Holm Cross** is a market town in the middle of the valley where the old drove roads met. It became
the railway junction, and the cooperative's engine works were built on its eastern edge.

**Oarside** is the harbour town at the mouth of the Lume. It has two quarters: the Quay, on the
harbour, and the Town, on the hill above it. The 1910 census counted 17,850 residents.

**Fallowmere** is a small upland town under the fells. Its slate quarry is the largest employer in
the upper valley. The 1910 census counted 3,420 residents.

Smaller places mentioned in other documents include Kestrel Wood, a hamlet in a wooded gorge between
Holm Cross and Oarside, and Lume Bridge, a farm settlement at the southern end of the viaduct.
